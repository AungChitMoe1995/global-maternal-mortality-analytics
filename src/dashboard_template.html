Change in aggregate MMR since 2000

MMR = maternal deaths per 100,000 live births. The aggregate uses summed country median death estimates and live births. It can differ from WHO's official global posterior estimate. Data snapshot: October 2026, MMEIG 2025 estimation round.

How did countries change?

Compare estimated mortality trajectories with their published 80% uncertainty intervals. Annual points may reflect modeled trends between direct observations.

Country averages and population burden differ

Each country has equal weight in the median. The aggregate ratio uses estimated maternal deaths and live births. Income labels use one acquisition-time classification throughout the historical series.

Logarithmic vertical axis. Cook Islands is unclassified in WDI and appears separately. Income groups describe countries using the snapshot classification, not their historical income tier.

How do national indicators vary together?

Each point represents one country. This view uses the balanced sample so its composition stays constant across years. A pattern between countries does not establish a mechanism.

Current health expenditure includes public, private and external financing. It does not isolate government budget priority or spending on maternal care. Total fertility does not measure individual parity or birth spacing.

Exploratory model results

The panel specifications adjust for fixed country differences and common year effects. Changing confounders, reverse causality and dependence on MMEIG's predictors remain.

What a coefficient means

For the log GDP predictor, the coefficient relates proportional GDP differences to proportional differences in estimated MMR, conditional on the specification. Other predictors enter in their displayed units. These estimates do not predict the effect of changing policy.

Panel confidence intervals use country-clustered standard errors and t inference. Cross-sectional models use HC1 standard errors. Income subsets have fewer country clusters.

What the sensitivity fits establish

The unbalanced panel, pre-pandemic sample, first differences and income subsets expose dependence on modeling choices. Replacing outcomes by marginal uncertainty limits is a sensitivity exercise. It does not propagate joint posterior uncertainty or create a credible interval for a regression coefficient.

A CRVS-only sensitivity analysis requires defensible country and period quality classifications and is not claimed here.

Data quality and research limits

Countries excluded from the balanced regression include several conflict-affected settings. The complete-case samples do not represent all countries equally. The descriptive views retain all available countries.

All sources are public national aggregates. The project adds no interpolation, while upstream series may be modeled or interpolated. A successful integrity audit does not validate the underlying estimates or remove bias.
